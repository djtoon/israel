## v3 acceptance

### Gameplay
- [x] **1. Core decision density.**
  - Core story decisions (forks, choice events, crises, war fronts, immigration, policy reviews, coalition; not ministers, budget or budget review) average **≥1.2 per turn**, with **≤30% empty turns**, in every decade from 1948 to 2026, as measured by the sim_test pacing report.
  - New decisions are real, sourced dilemmas (smaller ones count too: strikes, scandals, local crises, development projects).
  - Every non-historical option has a named advocate with a verbatim cite, and no option is dominated.
- [x] **2. The late game climaxes.**
  - The player's lead over history does not fade by default. A player who plays well should end ahead of the 1965 peak.
  - Late-game goals and projects exist: the tech economy, peace and normalisation deals, water independence (desalination), space and cyber, the gas fields.
  - Late surpluses can be turned into track gains.
- [x] **3. Endings and victory types.** At least 4 end states, for example a Secure State, a Prosperous State, Peace with Neighbours and a United Society, plus a "mixed record".
  - Each is decided by clear thresholds shown during play.
  - Each ending has an epilogue that reflects the player's actual choices (forks, wars, budgets).
  - The 2023–26 content gap is framed deliberately and neutrally (no deleted content returns).
- [x] **4. Diplomacy as a system.** The player manages relations with the US, USSR/Russia, France, the UK and Europe, Germany, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, the Gulf states, Turkey, Iran and the PLO/PA, each gated to its real era.
  - Each has a relation value with causes.
  - Each offers real deal types: aid, arms purchases, trade, treaties, back channels, recognition, sanctions risk.
  - Choices have trade-offs on the six tracks.
  - Historical treaties stay historical, but the player shapes their terms and timing where the forks allow.
  - There is a Civ VI–style diplomacy screen.
- [x] **5. Difficulty levels and balance.**
  - Easy, Normal and Hard (and Historian?) against the ghost, choosable at New Game.
  - Opposite strategies end at least 10 points apart overall in the divergence test.
  - No single dominant strategy across the varied, historical and strategist autoplayers.
- [x] **6. Scenarios.** Start in **1967** (after the Six-Day War), **1977** (the upheaval) or **1992** (Oslo era), as well as 1948. Each starts from a valid, historically seeded state (cities, borders, techs, treasury, relations) and plays to 2026, with its own short briefing.
- [x] **7. Smaller fixes:**
  - a debt and inflation indicator in the top bar;
  - the national "food surplus" reading made clear;
  - forks in 1951–52, 2007–08 and 2024–25 (kept topics only);
  - a real decision menu for Iran October 2024;
  - Koenig and Porush sourcing strengthened or reworded.

### Presentation
- [x] **8. Audio.** Generated with `tools/gen.py audio` (elevenlabs/music; see `audio_gen.md`):
  - in-game music by era (at least 8 eras × 2 tracks, 2–3 min, loopable), plus war and peace variants;
  - stingers (war begins, ceasefire or peace, election, new government, fork decided, research done, era change, game end);
  - ambient loops (coast, city, desert, Jerusalem);
  - UI and city SFX (click, hover, end turn, build complete, notification);
  - an `AudioDirector` with buses (Music, SFX, Ambience, UI), crossfades by era and situation, and no clipping.
- [x] **9. Art.**
  - **Portraits:** the advisor, a minister set by era, and every PM and key leader (stylized painted portraits, respectful, era-correct).
  - **Event illustrations:** at least 60 major events, painterly, no graphic violence; shown in the event popups and the Civilopedia.
  - **Hero buildings:** cleaner models for Tel Aviv's White City, Jerusalem's landmarks and the Haifa port and Bahá'í gardens; no soft or black models.
  - **Unit animations:** movement tweens, idle motion, and attack and hit feedback.
- [x] **10. Settings menu:**
  - Music, SFX, Ambience and UI volume;
  - UI scale (90–150%);
  - key rebinding;
  - colourblind-safe palette option;
  - the graphics preset (Low/Medium/High);
  - fullscreen/windowed;
  - language;
  - all saved to `user://game.cfg`.
- [x] **11. Hebrew UI.**
  - Every player-facing string goes through translation: UI, tutorial, How to Play, events, forks, techs, civics, buildings, units, places, the Civilopedia and endings.
  - Right-to-left layout works, with a Hebrew-capable font (OFL).
  - Choosable in Settings and on first launch.
  - Machine-translated by agents with a glossary of proper names (cities, people, parties, operations) in their standard Hebrew forms. A native-speaker review is left to the user (release quality).

- [x] **12. HD graphics mode** (accepted by the user 2026-10-13 after pass 2: rocky coasts, teal shelf, golden ground, sunlit vegetation; HD ~42–45 fps, SD unchanged). FROZEN: from now on HD gets bug fixes only (artefacts, black/floating models, SD/HD switch, fps); no new HD features or look changes. (added 2026-10-13 by the user). An "HD" setting reaches the look of `ref/hd_graphices.png`, following `ref/gadot_tips`, with NO new models: warm soft-shadow sun, GI, SSAO, PBR detail maps, a new water shader (ripples, reflections, depth colour, refraction, shoreline foam, reefs), backlit and varied vegetation, aerial fog, and AgX/ACES grading. "SD" is today's look. Both are switchable in Settings. HD holds ≥30 fps at 1080p on this laptop. A blind A/B critic judges it against the reference.

- [x] **13. Soft fail states** (the user's decision, 2026-10-13). There are early game-over endings for state collapse:
  - fiscal default: debt past the lenders' cap plus hyperinflation sustained for several years;
  - loss of public trust: repeated government collapses and mass unrest, with civic trust below a floor for years;
  - military collapse: readiness and losses past a breaking point during a war.

  Each has clear warnings shown well before the point of no return (top bar, advisor, an outlook panel) and a "what went wrong" epilogue. All are tuned so that no autoplayer (historical, varied, strategists, Recommended) ever triggers one on Normal, while deliberately bad play does. History stays a corridor: the user chose NOT to add alternate-history branching.
